A four-day school week with longer days would serve students better than the current five-day schedule. Longer class periods give teachers room for the lab work and discussion that a 45-minute block cuts short. In addition, the district's pilot at two middle schools reported fewer absences, because families scheduled appointments on the free day instead of pulling students out of class. Critics point out that "a day off is a childcare problem for working parents," and that concern is fair; the district can answer it by keeping buildings open on the fifth day for supervised study and clubs. When the tradeoffs are weighed, the longer school day buys more real learning time than the fifth day it replaces.
